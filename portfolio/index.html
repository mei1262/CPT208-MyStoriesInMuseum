Motivation & research

01

选题缘由（The Why）

We chose the Heritage (Digital Heritage & Play) track for A3 — My Stories in Museums because many visits remain linear and passive: visitors struggle to connect objects to personal meaning, while mobile guides rarely close the loop between onsite exploration and a shareable takeaway. Our formative survey work surfaced navigation fatigue, uneven interpretive depth, and limited post-visit reflection, alongside conditional interest in AI and AR when accuracy, privacy, and cognitive load were addressed. V-Museum responds by treating visitors as co-creators of a playful yet accountable heritage encounter, combining mobile-friendly XR cues, goal-driven route planning that makes trade-offs explicit, and AI-assisted commemorative storytelling such as short souvenir-style narratives or videos. We pair the visitor journey with a museum administration workflow so playful features remain institutionally grounded. Culturally resonant narration styles bridge expert interpretation and everyday curiosity, while layered depth supports families, students, and tourists with different time budgets. Finally, we treat playful features as optional, legible, and auditable by educators so digital heritage amplifies museums without displacing curatorial authority, aligning with CPT208 goals on equity, inclusion, and ethical computing. This stance ties personas, journey insights, prototypes, and evaluation to a single argument for responsible play in heritage settings.

Stakeholders & personas (The Stakeholders)

Primary user

Lin Xiao · Museum visitor (content experiencer)

Goals: Understand the visit path in limited time, reduce getting lost and information overload, and turn the visit into a story they can retell; open to light XR / AI support but needs clear flows and an easy way to exit.

Context: Weekends at a general museum with friends and a phone; wants a quick route plan before entry, spatial cues and playful prompts during the visit, and a shareable memento after leaving.

Pain points: Uneven label density, routes that feel generic, scattered memories after the visit; worries whether AI narration is trustworthy and whether it disturbs others.

What “playfulness” means: Light “play mode” choices via goal-based routes (e.g. deeper immersion vs. time-saving); small exploratory tasks or feedback on the floor; an emotional, customisable narrative or video recap at the end.

Secondary user

Zhou Lan · Museum digital ops / education specialist

Goals: Keep objects and digital content aligned, configure narration and events in the museum’s tone, and check that the visitor experience still matches curatorial intent.

Context: Updates objects, reviews public-facing copy and assets in the Museum Admin Center, and frequently adjusts routes and placements during temporary shows.

Pain points: Fragmented tools, visitor app and back office drifting out of sync, extra proofreading cost for AI-generated content.

Relationship to the system: Supplies trustworthy content and policy boundaries for the visitor side; pairs with the V-Museum visitor journey so “fun” does not trade off institutional responsibility or ethics.

User requirements

User journey map & pain points (User Journey Map)

Playfulness must-haves (3 Must-haves)

  1. 1. XR Immersive Historical Scene + AI Character Interaction
    • 1.1 Set up independent experience rooms to ensure a safe and stable experience.
    • 1.2 Restore historical scenes of cultural relics 1:1 through XR devices.
    • 1.3 Support real-time dialogue, puzzle solving, and task interaction with AI-driven virtual historical figures.
  2. 2. AI Co-created Memorial Video
    • 2.1 Visitors can capture wonderful moments during the visit through XR devices.
    • 2.2 AI automatically edits materials, integrating immersive scene clips and personal participation footage.
    • 2.3 Generate an exclusive customised memorial video, supporting one-click saving and sharing.
  3. 3. AI-Powered Personalized Visiting Adaptation
    • 3.1 AI dynamically adjusts explanation depth and visiting routes based on visitors’ interests and pace.
    • 3.2 Support digital check-in at key exhibits and accumulation of points.
    • 3.3 Achieve a personalised “thousand people, thousand faces” guidance experience.
    • 3.4 Exchange accumulated points for exclusive digital souvenirs or physical cultural and creative gifts.

Design alternatives (2–3 options)

During the early stages, we seriously considered two alternative approaches that leaned more heavily on a single technology:

Alternative Option A: Pure XR Historical Recreation

Focused on creating highly immersive, photorealistic recreations of historical environments and events, allowing visitors to “travel back in time.” It looked amazing, but it was pretty passive. There wasn’t much real interaction.

Alternative Option B: Pure AI Voice Chatbot Guide

Developing a knowledgeable, conversational AI voice assistant that visitors could freely question about any exhibit. It could have natural conversations and tailor explanations to each person. That made it more engaging and accessible, but it missed the emotional punch of actually seeing history come to life.

Final hybrid Option C (XR + AI) — adopted for this project

Advantages of the final hybrid option C (XR + AI):

The XR part builds the world and the atmosphere — it makes you feel like you’re really there in that historical moment. Then the AI voice turns that world into a conversation. Visitors can ask questions on the fly, ask for a simpler explanation, or dive deeper into something they’re curious about.

That turns a one-way tour into a kind of discovery game. It’s way less boring. Sure, building this hybrid system is more complicated than just picking one approach, but it’s worth it. Visitors remember more, get more curious, and have a better time — whether they’re kids, adults, or just have different ways of learning.

Evaluation & reflection

05

Usability testing (Alpha · three participants)

Once the full journey was demoable, we ran semi-structured interviews plus task walkthroughs: participants first worked alone through “browse overview → understand route / guide logic → imagine an on-site visit”, then we probed playfulness, clarity, network concerns, and privacy. This was a qualitative Alpha round without a formal SUS score; notes were thematically coded to feed architecture and UI priorities.

Key takeaway from Alpha (before case detail)

All three saw potential in personalised routes + narrative-style guidance, but agreed the main risk was weak in-museum networks: they would try it only if they were not “stuck waiting for loads in crowds”. That pushed us to move compute and content from always-on public cloud to local LLMs plus pre-installed resource packs, and to fetch large video / high-res assets asynchronously after the visit on a good connection.

受访者1

背景与动机
青年在校学生，常去城市博物馆；手机流量有限，对「现场连不上 Wi-Fi」有真实焦虑。
任务表现
能快速理解「我的路线」与讲解入口；在追问环节主动对比「在家流畅预览」与「馆内可能卡顿」的差异。
主要观点
强调可用性要先于炫技：若导览在排队或展厅深处断流，会立刻放弃使用。
观察笔记
多次复述「离线 / 本地」关键词；对「边缘设备」概念接受度高，更关心「是否提前下好包」。
匿名引语
「节假日博物馆网很差的话，会不会卡？要是能离线就更放心。」

受访者2

背景与动机
同龄朋友，偏好轻游戏化、短视频式信息节奏，反感长段纯文字展板。
任务表现
对互动节奏评价积极，能指出哪些模块「像在玩」、哪些仍偏说明文档。
主要观点
趣味来自反馈及时 + 叙事连贯；同时希望展后仍能「带走一点东西」（照片/小结），但接受「回家再下」。
观察笔记
在演示纪念/分享相关界面时停留较久，追问「会不会很大、要不要流量」。
匿名引语
「整体挺好玩的，不是光看字那种。」

受访者3

背景与动机
习惯提前做攻略；重视时间预算与「不走回头路」。
任务表现
优先阅读路线定制与历史游览模块；对信息层级是否一眼可扫非常敏感。
主要观点
认可可定制的导览动线是差异化卖点；建议弱网时仍保留「最低限度」的文本/图标兜底。
观察笔记
反复确认「到馆后第一步点哪里」；对网络问题的担忧弱于 P1，但更在意逻辑是否自洽。

Cross-participant themes

  • Playfulness: Narrative pacing and interaction received positive feedback; the “game-like” feel held up.
  • Adoption intent: All would try it on a real visit, provided weak networks do not break the core path.
  • Risks: Reliance on public internet, heavy assets, holiday congestion — aligning with respondent 1’s offline ask and respondent 2’s post-visit download preference.

Design and implementation responses (engineering)

Local LLM + resource packs (during the visit): Run the real-time guide model on venue AR devices / edge; wall text, structured object data, essential voice, and fallback assets ship as pre-installed or pre-visit bulk packages so core inference and playback stay mostly local, reducing dependence on in-building Wi-Fi / cellular quality.

Post-visit async cloud pulls (bandwidth safety): During the visit we can surface light previews and metadata; high-res photos, AI-rendered videos, and share cards download from a cloud queue after people leave and networks improve, avoiding contention with the critical on-floor path.

Route choice: The WebAR path that assumed continuous browser loads and public connectivity was deprioritised / retired in favour of assumptions that match venue devices + offline packs, consistent with the Alpha consensus that offline feels safer.

Iterative improvement (Before / After · feedback-driven)

UI layer: from scattered early screens to integrated flow + route customisation

The screenshots below are web prototype iterations; the architecture shift described above sits behind them: local LLM + pre-installed packs keep narration from stalling on weak networks, while heavy media use post-visit cloud sync — splitting “play on the floor” from “share afterward” into two bandwidth strategies.

Concrete optimisations (mapped to the figures)

  • Flow consolidation (respondent 3): Route and detail sit in one view to cut mid-visit hopping — each hop is extra latency and requests that hurt most on weak networks.
  • Weak-network first (respondent 1): Interaction assumes “packs ready + local LLM” instead of “always online fetch”; basic guidance can continue if the stream drops.
  • Defer heavy media (respondents 1 + 2): Final cuts and high-res cloud imagery move to post-visit download / background sync, balancing “I want a memento” with “the floor experience must stay stable”.

Societal / ethical impact and the role of AI (final reflection)

When V-Museum uses generative tools for narration style and commemorative media, we must govern factual accuracy (alignment with curatorial copy, review of sensitive wording), copyright and likeness (boundaries for portraits and AI re-rendering), and public audio and camera privacy (others overhearing guidance, field of view). The museum admin surface exists to keep “fun” inside auditable content boundaries so excitement about tech does not outrank inclusion (language, hearing, device variance) or respect for diverse audiences.

Generative AI in this project: We used AI for code scaffolding, copy polish, and visual sketch variants; requirements, interview synthesis, architecture trade-offs, and test logs were human-checked. We separated model suggestions from participant quotes and logged tools with access dates in References. A risk is that plausible model prose hides unverified claims — mitigations include quoting real participants, keeping Before/After and design alternatives visible, and stating weak-network engineering commitments rather than marketing-only claims.
